## Summary

Adds a community native adapter for **quipu**
([github.com/scbrown/quipu](https://github.com/scbrown/quipu)), a
governed bitemporal knowledge-graph store whose write gate emits three
evidence planes per decision: a writer-side **guard trace** (executor,
principal chain, tool, target, graph, instant), a signed **verdict
ledger** fact persisted in the store (policy, target, outcome, and the
write's attribution sealed inside the evidence hash its ed25519
signature covers), and a bitemporal **policy snapshot** (the policy's
claim as of the decision instant versus now, plus the writer's
authority grants).

Following CONTRIBUTING's "Adding a New Evidence Regime":

- `adapters/quipu.py` — `from_native_record` deriving the eight
  property categories from record content, plus a regime-internal
  source validator (field completeness, evidence-hash recomputation,
  executor–chain consistency, grant scope).
- `data/cases/quipu/missing_policy_001.native.json` — a native fixture
  exported from quipu's seeded, deterministic census benchmark (a real
  gate refusal), with the missing-policy degradation applied.
- Smoke-corpus coverage (case row, paired annotations, scorer output)
  and `tests/test_adapter_quipu.py`, including a test that a swapped
  attributed writer fails evidence-hash recomputation.

## Design decision: NATIVE_ADAPTERS, not REGIME_IDS

The adapter registers in `NATIVE_ADAPTERS` only. `REGIME_IDS` remains
the deposited eight-regime manuscript contract, so the 64-case corpus
arithmetic, draft generation, and all published results are unchanged;
promoting a ninth regime re-balances that arithmetic and reads like a
corpus-version decision for the maintainer, not a side effect of an
adapter PR. A registry comment documents the split. Smoke-corpus count
assertions grow 8 → 9 (64 → 72 property rows) accordingly.

`checksums.txt` is refreshed per CONTRIBUTING; this also repairs the
two rows (`pyproject.toml`, `__init__.py`) that were stale at HEAD —
happy to split that into its own commit if preferred.

## Motivation

We ran DEMM-Bench against quipu as a self-run ninth-regime extension in
the direction §9 F2 invites (harness and results:
[quipu/benchmark/demm](https://github.com/scbrown/quipu/tree/main/benchmark/demm)):
under the eight degradation conditions applied as content-level
deletions, a content-only reader of quipu's records reaches PSA 1.0
with zero overclaim, while the container-presence baselines overclaim
on up to 87.5% of the same cases — the container fallacy reproduced at
its ceiling on a store that always emits its containers. Upstreaming
the adapter lets those claims be re-derived inside this project's
harness instead of ours.
